3. RESEARCH BASIS

The controlling evaluation principle is established: selection over a finite development criterion can overfit the criterion and introduce substantial selection bias, even when the underlying fitting procedure is otherwise valid. Cawley and Talbot therefore support separating adaptive model development from a final external evaluation and reporting the unsuccessful result rather than selecting another model after seeing it. 
Journal of Machine Learning Research

Dwork and colleagues likewise show that repeated, adaptive reuse of a holdout can destroy statistical validity because later hypotheses depend on earlier holdout answers. Their work does not magically certify Gold37, nor is the reusable-holdout algorithm being used here; the relevant adaptation is the stricter operational principle: freeze V51 first, open Gold37 once, reveal no intermediate answers, and prohibit any V51 rescue after the result.
