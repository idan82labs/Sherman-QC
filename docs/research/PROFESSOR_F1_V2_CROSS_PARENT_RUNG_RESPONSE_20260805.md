4. Comparison with primary literature
Point-cloud crease and edge extraction

Gumhold, Wang, and MacLeod’s historical method constructs a point-neighbor graph, detects a crease pattern, groups neighborhoods along crease strips, fits two planes to the two groups, and takes their intersection as the local crease. It also recognizes that grouping becomes difficult near junctions and when unrelated or opposite faces contaminate the neighborhood. The attached paper therefore supports the importance of explicit two-side grouping and longitudinal strip evidence; it does not establish novelty for plane intersections, graph linkage, or wedge fitting, and its sampling assumptions should not be transferred directly to modern dense scans.
