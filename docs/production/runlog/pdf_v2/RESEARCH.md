# Formal dimensional inspection reports: findings (2026-10-19)

Short desk research for the C06 per-scan PDF redesign. Sources are listed at the end. AS9102 itself is SAE-copyrighted; the form layout below comes from the public IAQG blank forms and supplier templates.

1. **Every formal report opens with a title block in a fixed grid.** AS9102 Form 1 and supplier FAIR templates (Kaman, GD-OTS, Lockheed RMS) open with a boxed grid: part number, part name, revision, serial or lot, report or FAI number, organisation and customer, and an approval and date box. The fields keep fixed positions, so a reader finds the same field in the same place on every report.
2. **There is one row per characteristic, with a stable number.** AS9102 Form 3 numbers every characteristic ("Char. No."), and the number matches the drawing balloon. A reviewer can check completeness by comparing the highest number with the balloon count. In our report, each row's number combines the shop-order bend number and the characteristic's position within the bend (for example 3.2).
3. **The requirement is printed as drawn and the result as a measured value.** Form 3 asks for the nominal and tolerance with units under "Requirement", and the actual value (not "OK") under "Results". CALYPSO and PiWeb tables print Nominal, Upper and Lower tolerance, Actual and Deviation as separate numeric columns.
4. **There is a separate result column, and only it is coloured.** CALYPSO and PiWeb add a status or tolerance-usage bar per row. Out-of-tolerance rows are flagged in that column, and the rest of the table stays neutral, so the flags stand out.
5. **The verdict summary sits on page 1.** PiWeb's standard and interactive protocols put a summary near the top of page 1: characteristic counts in, near and out of tolerance, plus a CAD overview that marks where the out-of-tolerance features are. The detail tables follow.
6. **A numbered 3D or drawing overview links the table to the part.** The ZEISS interactive protocol (2017+) places a CAD model on page 1 with characteristic markers. For press-brake parts the markers are the bend numbers (shop order and studio label).
7. **The sign-off is a human box, not a machine verdict.** FAIR forms end with an inspector name, signature and date, and a disposition. In our report this box makes the operator-assist rule visible: the software reports, and the inspector decides.
8. **Every page carries a running header and footer.** Templates repeat the part number, revision, report number, "Page X of Y" and a controlled-document or confidentiality line on every page. This lets a single printed sheet be traced back to its report. SOLIDWORKS Inspection and PolyWorks ReportLoop both use corporate templates with fixed header and footer rows.
9. **Method, uncertainty and raw evidence go in an appendix.** Formal reports keep the body to the requirement and the result. Measurement method, registration and alignment, uncertainty and decision rules (here the guard band |dev| ± U against the tolerance) go in a small-type appendix or a separate CMM report referenced from Form 3.
10. **Typography and colour are restrained.** Templates use one sans family, black and grey rules on a consistent column grid, generous cell padding, and the company colour only in the title block. Red, amber and green appear only on result cells. Forum threads also show that tolerance flags must come from the stored result, never from re-rounded numbers (CALYPSO and PiWeb flag disagreements at the limits caused by rounding). The report therefore prints the engine's own decision and does not recompute it.
