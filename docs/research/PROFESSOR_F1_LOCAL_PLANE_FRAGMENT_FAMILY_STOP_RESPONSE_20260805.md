INFERENCE
1. Missing cross-parent connectivity was not the operative bottleneck

The intervention’s causal premise was that eligible adjacent parents on opposite fixed fragments were insufficiently linked, and that adding complete, non-top-k cross-parent rungs could connect the relevant support and advance bilateral geometry.

The experiment distinguishes two possibilities:

The rungs never touch relevant targets.
This is false for 122 targets.

The rungs touch target components but do not improve bilateral support.
This occurred for all 122 reached targets.

Accordingly, under the frozen fragments, pair axes, tube memberships, bins, and gates, the absence of these cross-parent edges was not the bottleneck responsible for side-gate failure.

This does not prove which element is the bottleneck. Possibilities outside the tested delta include the frozen plane estimates, fragment partition, parent-to-pair membership, support localization, axial discretization, or the representation of a bend as a component over two fixed fragments. Identifying among those would require a materially new hypothesis rather than another execution of this intervention.

2. Designated replays cannot rescue the ordinary objective

A designated replay could test repeatability or detect nondeterminism. It cannot convert the source-rebuilt ordinary output from zero side advances to a positive mechanistic result without changing inputs or semantics.

Likewise, the full synthetic-resource envelope would address scaling and structural caps. It would not establish that the ordinary intervention advances target geometry on the frozen 37 cases.

Authorizing either lane after 0 / 122 exposed-target advancements would therefore test robustness or resource behavior of an empirically inactive mechanism, not its ordinary efficacy. That is not a justified continuation.

3. A post hoc failure audit would be descriptive, not a family-saving experiment

A further report could count first-rejection codes or bin deltas, but it would not change the established result that the authorized intervention failed at its immediate preregistered mechanism. Treating such an audit as another open “next step” would turn a complete zero-effect ordinary result into an indefinite rescue funnel.

Any later proposal that changes fragments, planes, pair membership, support representation, gate semantics, or feature construction must be treated as a new representation/intervention family. It must not inherit authorization from this stopped profile.

Relationship to established research

Robust plane fitting is an established primitive. Fischler and Bolles introduced RANSAC for robust model fitting, and Schnabel, Wahl, and Klein subsequently applied efficient RANSAC to primitive detection in unorganized point clouds. Those methods establish that planes and other primitives can be recovered robustly; they do not establish that a particular plane-fragment component graph represents physical bends correctly. DOI: 10.1145/358669.358692; DOI: 10.1111/j.1467-8659.2007.01016.x. 
ACM Digital Library
+1

Region growing likewise depends jointly on local surface estimates, neighborhood connectivity, and thresholds. Rabbani, van den Heuvel, and Vosselman explicitly formulate smoothness-constrained point-cloud segmentation using local normals plus point connectivity and describe a parameter-dependent tradeoff between under- and over-segmentation. Therefore, exact reproduction of fitted planes and graph inputs does not imply that the resulting regions or cross-crease support are semantically correct. 
ISPRS

Graph connectivity is an established segmentation mechanism: Felzenszwalb and Huttenlocher formulate segmentation through graph edges and a boundary predicate. In the present experiment, edges did affect connectivity, as shown by target reach and the component-count reduction, but graph connectivity alone did not satisfy the downstream bilateral geometric predicate. DOI: 10.1023/B:VISI.0000022288.19776.77. 
Springer

Sharp-feature and crease extraction from point clouds is also established. Demarsin and colleagues combine normal estimation with graph theory to extract closed sharp feature lines. That work reinforces the distinction exposed here: graph linkage is one ingredient, but edge geometry also depends on the underlying normal/surface evidence and feature-line construction. Adding a rung without changing planes, points, normals, pair membership, or side-bin evidence cannot be assumed to create the missing crease support. DOI: 10.1016/j.cad.2006.12.005. 
ScienceDirect

Finally, the deletion test supports a narrowly computational causal statement. Pearl’s intervention framework distinguishes causal effects under a specified model from ordinary observational association. Here the model-level intervention is explicit—add the rung table, then delete it and rebuild—but the model does not include physical bend truth. The valid conclusion is therefore that these rungs caused no preregistered novel target certificate within the frozen computation, not that the represented components are physically correct or incorrect bends.
