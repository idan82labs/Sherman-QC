Causal hypothesis
A true sheet-metal bend contains a connected band of single-surface neighborhoods with exactly two stable infinitesimal slippable rigid motions:


translation along one axis;


rotation about that same axis.


Transversely, that two-motion band must terminate on both sides in three-motion planar neighborhoods whose normals are nonparallel and whose cross product agrees with the recovered screw axis.
A complete cylinder has the two-motion signature everywhere but has no bilateral planar tails. A flat sheet is three-slippable rather than two-slippable. A hole wall has cylindrical slippage, but its adjacent planar normals are parallel to the hole axis and occur radially rather than on the two circumferential sides. Terminal walls, spheres, generic doubly curved surfaces, and sharp joins fail either the two-motion signature or the bilateral-tail geometry.
This is causally distinct from every named closed family:

ESTABLISHED MECHANISM
Gelfand and Guibas define an infinitesimal rigid motion by v(x) = r cross x + t and call the motion slippable when its normal component vanishes over the surface. A plane admits two tangent translations and a rotation about its normal; a cylinder admits translation along its axis and rotation about that axis. Guibas Lab
For a point neighborhood, they reduce the tangency objective to a symmetric 6 by 6 matrix assembled from point-normal terms. Near-null eigenvectors of that matrix form the local slippage signature. Their formulation applies to unorganized point clouds after local normal estimation and centers and rescales each neighborhood for numerical conditioning. Guibas Lab+1
They report classifying an eigenvalue as small using an inverse eigenvalue ratio between 100 and 300. LSSC freezes the reciprocal of 250, namely 0.004, and adds a separate 0.008 hysteresis wall. The multiscale schedule, screw-axis extraction, bilateral planar bracketing, graph construction, source-free fixtures, and all fund/kill criteria are new Sherman-specific hypotheses, not published bend-detection results. Guibas Lab
