1. Mechanism and intended observable

For a candidate axis a, define the corresponding great circle of unoriented surface normals as

G(a)={n∈S
2
:n⋅a=0}.

Because scan normals are unoriented, n and −n are identified. The normal field therefore lives in the projective sphere, and angular position along G(a) is represented modulo π.

The single defect-relevant observable is:

A source-connected ribbon whose unoriented normals traverse a continuous great-circle arc while the ribbon remains repeatedly elongated along the common axis a.

For a straight sheet-metal bend, the Sherman-specific hypothesis is that normals rotate across the bend but remain approximately constant along its longitudinal direction. A candidate is therefore not accepted merely because many normals lie near a great circle. Its source-space preimage must form a coherent ruled ribbon, and that ribbon must subsequently pass the exact frozen parent, tube, pair-support, side-gate, and attribution predicates.

The prior CIIP miss margin of 0.048610888363373 mm is not imported as a tolerance, score, or success condition. PGRR must pass the existing frozen support predicate exactly.

2. Research basis and its limits

Peternell’s point-cloud work represents a developable surface through a one-parameter family of tangent planes. For a general cylinder with axis direction a, the tangent planes are parallel to a, so their normals satisfy n⋅a=0; the corresponding tangent-plane image is curve-like rather than two-dimensional. That establishes the geometric basis for looking for a one-dimensional normal-space arc and a common ruling direction. It does not establish that such an arc will detect Sherman bends or improve F1.

Three roles run sequentially.

Producer A

A native deterministic implementation using:

its own exact spatial index;

fixed-order covariance accumulation;

a fixed symmetric eigensolver;

union-find source components;

bitset target intersections;

intermediate-node graph augmentation.

Producer A-PERM

The same implementation receives a deterministic SHA-256 permutation of the input rows before canonicalization. It must produce a byte-identical producer bundle after canonicalization.

Validator B

A separately implemented validator that:

does not import, link, or call producer geometry code;

uses a different spatial-index implementation;

uses LAPACK/NumPy-style symmetric eigenanalysis rather than the producer eigensolver;

uses sparse-graph connected components rather than producer union-find;

regenerates the icosphere from the fixed icosahedral face list instead of trusting the producer pole table;

independently rebuilds candidate memberships from the raw source;

writes its candidate commitment before being given access to target rows;

independently constructs target masks, chains and central cuts;

independently rebuilds all four target-outcome tables.

The validator does not read producer candidate rows until its own semantic bundle has been committed.

Numeric disagreements

Any producer/validator disagreement or any decisive predicate within 32 ULP of its boundary is recomputed from the original binary64 coordinates using at least 160-bit arithmetic.

A uniquely resolved result becomes normative.

An exact or unresolved boundary produces PGRR_V1_NUMERIC_BOUNDARY_INVALID_STOP.

No epsilon is widened.
