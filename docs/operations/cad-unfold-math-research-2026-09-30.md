# CAD unfolding: mathematical decomposition research — 2026-09-30

## Recommendation

Separate **surface carrier**, **topological role**, and **output promise**. Preserve the incumbent exact extrusion certificate. Add a separately versioned native boundary-component/skin-ownership proof before tessellation: a spline wall can be excluded boundary material without itself admitting the selected skin's flattening chart. Support stepped machining features only with an explicit footprint/feature ledger, not by pretending their walls are uniform through-holes. Keep manufacturing blank generation a separate neutral-surface mechanism with process inputs.

This is source-only research. No CAD/corpus, scan, raw, held-out, email, Gold, manifest, authorization or historical execution artifact was accessed. No native geometry or tests were executed. Availability above 90% is an evaluation target, not a consequence of these equations or an established result. Historical 3/5 availability remains a consumed-development preview observation, not bend accuracy.

## Source diagnosis

Reviewed canonical instructions/current status/repository map/Astra workflow and named source seams. `backend/vendor_surface_normalization_v1.py::_direction_proof` proves a particularly useful sufficient case: positive tensor-product weights independent of the extrusion parameter, a common monotone scalar displacement sequence, constant generator direction, and a transverse base. It computes physical trimmed displacement by de Boor, rather than multiplying parameter length by endpoint length. Unequal generator profiles, coupled weights and folding control sequences correctly fail that certificate. Failure does not prove the surface is nondevelopable or not a cut wall.

`backend/vendor_unfold_native_v5.py::read_native_v5` requests a surface descriptor for every face before building the native edge inventory or tessellating. Consequently, an unsupported wall carrier can stop the import before topology establishes that it is excluded from the skin. This ordering is the first architecture seam to change in a successor, not a reason to loosen spline recognition.

The incumbent V3 lives in `backend/vendor_unfold_preview.py` (not a `_v3.py` file). `_skin_graph` validates paired coaxial cylinders, radii, angular/axial trims and a globally consistent thickness inferred from radius differences. `_select_skin` requires complete tree coverage; `_verify_skin_boundary` requires exactly two complete skin components and explains every native face. Its curved boundary branch assumes one cylindrical/extruded wall joins translated opposite rails and spans exactly the thickness. That is stronger than the boundary-component condition needed for a split wall, but intentionally refuses shoulders/bosses that would otherwise disappear.

`backend/vendor_unfold_preview_v4.py` adds finite-width guard development; V5 stitches native coplanar charts and replaces the guard with local finite-strip evidence. V5 preserves source slices and coherent positive-Z exported triangle winding. `backend/vendor_unfold_oracle_bridge_v1.py` explicitly reports continuous area checking as not established. A discrete triangle/metric certificate must not silently become a continuous trim proof.

Existing `tests/test_vendor_surface_normalization_v1.py` covers degree-elevated/nonlinear trims, transposition, rational transverse weights, pose/mirror invariance, knot insertion and adversarial coefficient error. These tests construct native surfaces and were inspected only, not run here.

## Three distinct geometry problems

1. **Carrier normalization:** identify the mathematical locus independent of STEP representation. A rational B-spline can encode an analytic plane/cylinder or a general ruled surface. Representation name is not physical role.
2. **Sheet ownership:** identify two oriented material skins, their local correspondence and intervening boundary components; account for every face. Tangency and thinness are necessary evidence, not sufficient proof of sheet membership.
3. **Flattening/manufacturing:** prove a selected skin is intrinsically flat and globally chartable, or choose a neutral-surface process model. These outputs have different dimensions and uncertainty.

OCCT's [BRepAdaptor_Surface](https://dev.opencascade.org/doc/refman/html/class_b_rep_adaptor___surface.html) provides typed surfaces, parameter bounds and derivatives. Its [Geom_SurfaceOfLinearExtrusion](https://dev.opencascade.org/doc/refman/html/class_geom___surface_of_linear_extrusion.html) is generated by translating a curve along a fixed direction. These are useful carrier facts, not certificates of solid ownership or manufacturing intent. Face-local locations, orientation, native pcurves, seam uses and wires must be retained separately.

## Exact math and safe generalizations

For a regular ruled patch

\[
X(u,v)=c(u)+v d(u),\quad X_u=c'(u)+v d'(u),\quad X_v=d(u),
\]

developability requires

\[
\det(c'(u),d(u),d'(u))=0
\]

throughout the trimmed interval, with `|X_u × X_v| > 0` on the regular domain. Straight generators alone do not imply zero Gaussian curvature: a twisted ruled patch is a counterexample. See [Patrikalakis–Maekawa–Cho, developable surface geometry](https://web.mit.edu/hyperbook/Patrikalakis-Maekawa-Cho/node190.html). For paired boundary curves `a,b`, the equivalent determinant is `det(a-b,a',b')=0`; [Tang et al., Interactive design of developable surfaces](https://www.geometrie.tugraz.at/wallner/abw.pdf) develops spline formulations. A few small sampled determinants are screening evidence only. For rational spline spans, form polynomial homogeneous derivative numerators, certify the determinant and regularity using Bernstein bounds/subdivision with finite depth and report the remaining uncertainty. Exact floating-point zero comparisons are not universal algebraic equality proofs.

There are two different meanings of varying generators:

- `X(u,v)=C(u)+h(u,v)n`, with fixed `n`, varying length/parameterization: this can still lie on an extrusion carrier. The incumbent separable `C(u)+g(v)n` is sufficient, not necessary. A successor can certify projection `P_n X` independent of `v`, regular monotone fiber traversal, and actual boundary intersections. For a planar skin pair, all physical fibers must bridge separation `t`, even when parameter endpoints vary with `u`.
- `X(u,v)=c(u)+v d(u)` with genuinely varying directions: it may be developable, twisted, or a boundary wall around a bent skin. Developability is relevant only if this patch is selected for flattening. Boundary-role proof instead needs ownership and fiber/trim correspondence; a ruled label alone is never enough.

For a sheet parameterization `p` and unit normal `n`, paired offsets satisfy `p_±=p±(t/2)n`. With shape operator convention `dn=-S dp`, the local volume map `F(p,s)=p+s n` has tangential Jacobian `det(I-sS)=(1-sκ1)(1-sκ2)`. Require nonvanishing Jacobian and global absence of colliding fibers, not merely a small normal-distance residual. Plane skins require separation `t`; coaxial cylindrical skins require radial difference `t`. Offset regularity fails at focal distances. Matching nearby surfaces without trim coverage and uniqueness can misclassify unrelated pockets.

For a cylinder of selected radius `R`, an exact skin chart is `Φ(θ,z)=(R(θ-θ0),z)` because the metric is `R²dθ²+dz²`. Planes use proper rigid charts. General patches need `DΦᵀDΦ=I_first`, continuity on the complete native seams, compatible orientation, and global injectivity of the retained domain. A spanning tree determines propagation but says nothing about an omitted cycle: every non-tree seam must independently close. Genuine cycle inconsistency needs an explicit cut/rip policy or refusal, not arbitrary spanning-tree omission.

## Boundary components and stepped features

A relevant primary precedent is [Yang, Hinduja, Owodunni and Heinemann, Recognition of features in sheet metal parts manufactured using progressive dies, CAD 134 (2021) 102991](https://quaoar.su/files/papers/sheet_metal/j.cad.2021.102991.pdf). Their method extracts connected thickness-face chains before classifying topological/geometric feature characteristics, under a stated constant-sheet-thickness assumption. Their cut-feature characteristic uses vertices on two thickness-offset planes. This supports component-level decomposition as an architecture pattern, not a sufficient arbitrary-spline wall certificate: vertex positions alone cannot bound the intervening surface or exclude folded generators. Our proposed coefficient/normal-fiber proof is deliberately stronger. Their feature-recognition results do not establish this repository's extraction accuracy or manufacture-ready blanks.

A safe generalized boundary proof operates on connected excluded face components, not a one-face neighbor:

- Every source face has exactly one role; each edge use has complete oriented incidence, including seams/degenerate uses.
- A component meets the two skins along complete corresponding boundary cycles; internal split edges cancel. No additional forming surface, island or branching material is hidden inside it.
- Certified normal/radial fibers span the material thickness and preserve trim correspondence; component interiors stay within the appropriate swept slab, not a universal planar halfspace.
- Holes, outer boundaries and nesting are distinguished by oriented cycles on the skin chart. Each boundary has a continuous native curve receipt and a bounded polygonal approximation if exported as mesh.
- Proof budgets and typed failure reasons remain immutable; unproved faces still stop a complete output.

Topology alone does not prove constant thickness or absence of undercuts. It establishes which analytic conditions must be checked. A varying spline wall with translated complete rails might pass this proof; no claim is made that either currently refused CAD actually satisfies it.

A counterbore/countersink can leave two selected skins with different hole footprints. The solid boundary includes a shoulder and multiple wall pieces. An exact selected-skin preview can retain the chosen footprint and publish the opposite footprint plus shoulder as a machining overlay, provided ownership is completely established. It cannot simultaneously claim a single through-hole cutting outline. A local boss may be machined from thick stock, attached, or formed by plastic flow; final B-rep alone does not establish the original blank or process. A formed doubly curved dome is not isometric to a plane and must refuse exact development. Stretch-forming simulation is a separate product.

## FreeCAD comparison and neutral-axis boundary

The maintained [FreeCAD SheetMetal new unfolder source](https://github.com/shaise/FreeCAD_SheetMetal/blob/master/SheetMetalNewUnfolder.py) is a practical comparison: it estimates thickness from normal edges, face evidence or cylinders; uses typed tangency comparisons; flattens cylindrical pcurves; and handles bend allowance through a dedicated calculator with ANSI/DIN K-factor conversion. Some surface tangency fallbacks sample a linear edge midpoint. These are useful engineering patterns, not independent certification for this repository; do not transplant a sampled test as a global proof. Its warning/refusal paths also show that accepting a tangent surface does not mean all such surface types have a robust exact manufacturing development.

For inner bend radius `Ri`, thickness `t`, neutral-axis fraction `k` and angular sweep `α` in radians,

\[
BA=α(R_i+kt).
\]

The selected outer-skin preview uses `α(Ri+t)`; the inner skin uses `αRi`. Their difference is `αt`. Neither is generally the material's neutral-axis allowance. The [Autodesk sheet-metal unfold reference](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2014/ENU/Inventor/files/GUID-2B82E54A-2A8A-4F99-8647-55405D373747.htm) documents radius/thickness/K-factor allowance. CAD shape alone supplies no uniquely calibrated `k`: material, tooling/process, bend history and chosen compensation convention remain external inputs. Do not use `k=0.5` as an unstated truth. Verified units and process-calibrated uncertainty must accompany any manufacturing output.

Tests should use independently authored coefficients and symbolic/analytic answers, not candidate-generated expected receipts. Keep preview availability, bend extraction precision/recall, continuous geometry validity, and manufacturing dimensional agreement in separate ledgers; retain all abstentions in their relevant denominators.

This first contract deliberately certifies a **complete polygonal single planar slab**, not an entire bent part. It admits positive-weight rational tensor patches whose generator speed varies with the transverse coordinate, while requiring fixed normal fibers and constant physical endpoint levels, and supports axial splitting into multiple faces. It checks full native edge-curve coefficients, coherent two-owner incidence, full ordered skin wires, matching skin footprints, polygon/hole simplicity and orientation, complete nonoverlapping normal-fiber intervals and ownership of every source face. Curved outlines, partial rectangular trims, multipair global geometry and stepped shoulders remain typed refusals. Local coefficient bounds are explicitly not physical or continuous-trim accuracy.

Before runtime use, a separately reviewed native adapter must establish byte/source binding, exact full-chart trim coverage from pcurves/wires (surface UV min/max alone is insufficient), analytic located plane skin carriers, complete located/oriented native inventory and valid single-solid ownership. Native evidence must be extracted internally, never accepted as client-supplied receipt fields. Integration into a bent-sheet pipeline must certify every complementary forming-skin/cap component and retain the complete source-face ledger; this limited slab certificate cannot justify ignoring the rest of a part. No native adapter or V6 integration is supplied by this checkpoint.
