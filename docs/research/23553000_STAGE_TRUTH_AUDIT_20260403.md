## Key observation

`SCAN_1` is not behaving like a noisy unstable detector result.

The six currently formed bends on `SCAN_1` are stable into later stages. There are no scan-1-only bends that disappear later.

That means the remaining `SCAN_1` discrepancy is not best explained by:
- random detector noise
- poor scan completeness
- simple threshold drift

It is a stage-truth interpretation problem.

## Why `CAD_B8` and `CAD_B13` are the best scan-1 suspects

`CAD_B8`
- formed on `SCAN_1`
- very weak side balance on `SCAN_1`: `0.011`
- only continuity-carried on `SCAN_2`
- fully observed on the final scan
- fits a "later bend seen too early" interpretation

`CAD_B13`
- formed on `SCAN_1`
- strongly observed on `SCAN_1`
- strongly observed on `SCAN_2`
- strongly observed on the final scan
- still, if the user count `4` is correct, `CAD_B13` must be one of the bends that should not be counted on stage 1

So:
- `CAD_B8` is the easier semantic suspect
- `CAD_B13` is the stronger geometric result but still the only clean way to reconcile stage counts without changing the user truth

## Engineering conclusion

The kept `CAD_B12` patch is good.

Reason:
- it corrects a stage-2 undercount
- it does not rely on the unresolved `SCAN_1` truth ambiguity
- it matches the best-fit stage model above

The unresolved `SCAN_1` residual should not be solved with another generic threshold.

It now requires one of two things:
1. bend-id stage labels from the source truth
2. a narrowly justified stage-semantics rule that suppresses `CAD_B8` and probably `CAD_B13` on stage 1
